# Reflection

**Most difficult concept.** The hardest idea for me was concurrency: how a database stays correct when many people act at the same moment. At first I thought checking "is the seat free?" and then saving the booking was enough. I only understood the problem when I traced two users through those steps by hand and saw both pass the check before either one saved. What helped me was drawing timelines of two transactions side by side, then rewriting the check and the update as one atomic statement and adding a unique constraint as a safety net. Designing TicketHub's seat holds made the idea concrete, because I had to explain exactly why a second buyer gets a clean "seat unavailable" instead of the same seat.

**What I would improve in my capstone.** The feedback I received pointed to error handling and to explaining my design decisions. My capstone works on the happy path, but it does not always give users clear messages when something fails, and my documentation says what I built without saying why. I would add input validation, consistent error responses, and a short design section that records my choices and trade-offs, the way I did in this design document.

**What I will learn next.** I want to learn how to deploy and run applications reliably: Docker, cloud hosting, caching and monitoring. I also want to learn more about testing under load, so I can check whether a design really survives a busy day instead of only reasoning about it. Later, I would like to combine this with my AI training and build systems that are both intelligent and dependable.
